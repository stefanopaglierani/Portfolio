Where this comes from

In 2015 I completed an 800-hour vocational program through CESCOT Italia and earned a certification as an Industrial Automation Technician, with a focus on programmable logic controllers (PLC). That training is the real foundation of my interest in automation.

Today I work in operations at SCM Group, a leading Italian manufacturer of industrial CNC and woodworking machinery. My day-to-day role is logistics and warehouse operations, not software engineering, but it keeps me close to real manufacturing floors and to the kind of equipment my automation training was built around.

Where it's going

  • Rebuilding programming skills. I'm re-strengthening the scripting and software side of automation, primarily in Python, using AI-assisted development to move faster while I rebuild syntax fluency.
  • Programming and debugging logic. The core skill I've kept from my technical-school background is the ability to reason through a system, structure it cleanly, and find what's actually broken. That transfers directly from PLC logic to general-purpose software.
  • The goal. A role where I can combine an operations-level understanding of manufacturing environments with software and automation work, rather than staying purely on the warehouse floor.

The habits from the factory show in the software I build: Edge Sentinel runs unattended and fails safely, Cozy Ambience is a production line that runs on a schedule, and Universal Game Tools refuses jobs that would overload the machine.

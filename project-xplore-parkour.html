XPLORE Parkour app

A mobile-first web app for the members and staff of XPLORE Parkour, a parkour school with about 400 members in Rimini and Pesaro, Italy, plus its public website. App and site share one account, one rules engine and one database.

The brief

A real school, real members, real money: bookings, memberships paid in instalments and the tax receipts Italian parents need for the sports-expense deduction.

I defined the product together with the gym, and the beta covers what members and staff do every week.

For members

  • Weekly timetable per site with live free spots; book and cancel within the weekly quota of the plan.
  • Memberships by season, paid in 1, 3, 6 or 12 instalments, with a 10-day grace period before an account is blocked.
  • An automatic tax receipt for every payment, with the data required by Italian law for 5–18 year olds, generated as a PDF on the device.
  • A community feed, unlocked after an introduction approved by staff, and private messages with request, accept and block.
  • Level Up: the gym's paper booklet of 36 challenges made digital; a new badge flashes until it's opened.
  • Italian and English, and sign-in to the website by scanning a QR code with the app.

For staff

  • Roll call with present and absent, approvals and member search.
  • Managers activate memberships, register payments at the desk, extend due dates, change roles and suspend accounts.
  • Coaches can award a challenge only on a day the member actually booked, and leave a note the member can read.
  • Every role is limited to its own site: a Pesaro coach never sees Rimini's members.

One engine, three places

The key architectural decision: the rules engine is written once and runs everywhere. It receives its database, crypto functions and clock from outside, so the same code runs:

On the serverSQLite on disk, Node's crypto
In the browsersql.js, for a full demo that needs no server
In the testsIn-memory database and a fake clock that jumps weeks ahead

The fake clock is what makes overdue instalments and account blocks testable in milliseconds instead of waiting ten days. The interface never decides anything that matters: it asks the engine, and the engine always checks ownership, role and site. Prices are computed on the server.

The website

One long page whose background is a film of the gym: the video moves forward and backward as you scroll. It shares the timetable, events and Level Up with the app, and the same account.

Reviewed before beta

Before presenting the beta I ran dedicated design, accessibility and security reviews, and tested every flow on desktop, phone and tablet.

  • Passwords stored as scrypt hashes with a random salt and checked in constant time; session tokens stored only as SHA-256 hashes.
  • All SQL uses bound parameters; uploads of SVG files are rejected; no cookies, so no cross-site request forgery surface.
  • Sessions are revoked on password change, password reset and suspension.
  • The security review found a real privilege-escalation bug (a site manager could reset the head office password). It was fixed and is now covered by a test.

Honest status. Not production-ready yet: online payments are simulated, login has no rate limiting, and there is no production server. Those are the first items on the roadmap, followed by store apps with push notifications.

Try it

The repository includes a demo with 11 accounts, from a member with an overdue instalment to the head office. Open the app in two browsers with different accounts and watch a coach award a challenge while the member's badge turns gold.

git clone https://github.com/stefanopaglierani/xplore-parkour-app.git
cd xplore-parkour-app
npm install
npm run dev:demo
